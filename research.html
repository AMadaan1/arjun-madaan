Physics-informed liquid neural networks for early sepsis prediction

Sepsis is survivable if it's caught early, and the signal is present in vitals hours before a clinician would call it. The modelling problem is unpleasant in three specific ways: the time series are irregularly sampled, the physiology is continuous while the records are not, and the positive class is rare enough that a model can score well by predicting that nobody is ever septic.

I'm working on a hybrid architecture that pairs a liquid neural network, handling irregular sampling by design, with physics-informed constraints that penalise physiologically impossible trajectories. The hypothesis is that the combination of the two will create a model that saves clinicians time, and engineers/researchers data.

This is independent research under the mentorship of a professor at Virginia Commonwealth University, and it is ongoing.

Sampling temperature and LLM accuracy on physics problems

During Brown's Smartphone and Computational Physics program, I led a group studying how sampling temperature affects the accuracy of language models on complex physics problems — the trade between deterministic decoding and the exploration that multi-step reasoning sometimes needs. I chose the topic and directed the group's work.

The instructor, Professor Renuka Rajapakse of UMass Dartmouth, wrote in our performance report that the group presented an excellent evaluation of how well generative AI solved complex physics problems, and that the findings were useful to her as a physics teacher.
